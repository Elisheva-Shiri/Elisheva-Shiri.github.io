## The exercise

In the course _Material, Culture, Research_, every object has two biographies. The
**archetypal biography** tells the story of the material: where it comes from and how it is made.
The **private biography** follows one particular object through the lives it has lived.

## The archetypal biography

A porcupine quill begins as hair. The Indian crested porcupine's skin produces **keratin**, the
same protein as our hair and nails, which hardens into sharp, hollow quills that protect the
animal. Each quill has a pale shell, an inner fibrous structure and a darker tip.

## The private biography

This quill fell out on the Hebrew University campus in Jerusalem, where porcupines are a familiar
night-time sight. From there it began a series of new lives:

1. **Formation** in the body of the porcupine.
2. **Fallout** in the campus “forest”.
3. **A bookmark**, about 10 cm long, sticking out of both sides of the book.
4. **A hairpin makeover.** After years of use, the quill split in two, and one half became a
   hairpin about 5 cm long.

## What it shows

The object stays the same, but each new use gives it a new meaning, from protection to memory to
adornment.
