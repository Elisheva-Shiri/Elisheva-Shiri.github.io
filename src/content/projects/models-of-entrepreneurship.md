## The question

What makes an entrepreneur successful? This essay was the final paper for a course on **models of
entrepreneurship and management through the philosophy of science**, taught by Dr. Yaron
Cohen-Tzemach.

## A short history

There have always been entrepreneurs, people who created change when they thought it could make
life easier. The word *entrepreneur* appears in the 14th century. Richard **Cantillon** was the
first to use it often and in its modern sense: for him, the entrepreneur is an innovator who
breaks the existing order. If you kept the order, you would stay in your village. Deciding to get
up and go is what disturbs the status quo, and it is how market towns grow.

For a long time, economics tried to explain the entrepreneur away. Only in the 20th century,
with Joseph **Schumpeter** (1912) and his theory of economic development, did the entrepreneur
become central. Courses in entrepreneurship began in the 1970s.

## The essay

The essay looks at entrepreneurial thinking from two angles: the kinds of entrepreneurship, and
how they connect with philosophical thinking. It does this through the lives of entrepreneurs who
changed the world, such as Bill Gates and George Soros. It opens with Kant's categorical
imperative: act only as if the principle behind your action were to become a universal law of
nature.
