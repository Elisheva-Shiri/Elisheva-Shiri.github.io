## The problem

South Korea drinks a lot of coffee: on average 12.3 cups per person a week, about 26.5 billion
cups a year. Most of them are disposable. The government has banned plastic cups for customers
who sit in cafés and plans to end single-use products, but enforcement is weak. Small cafés also
find it hard to compete with the big chains.

## The solution

**CoPPeeCUP** (Coffee Cup, "go pick up") is a reusable, designed cup that becomes a brand of its
own:

- **Personal.** Customers choose the colour, size and shape, and decorate the cup with stickers,
  magnets and charms.
- **Smart.** A barcode on the cup identifies the user. Every drink bought in the cup earns
  points, discounts and a higher "green" level.
- **Social.** The app maps the participating cafés and lets you share photos, recommend places,
  meet friends, order ahead and pay.
- **Green.** Mini-games and "did you know?" facts raise environmental awareness.

## The business

The main income comes from cafés (B2B: advertising and commission), and more from users (B2C:
branded cups and features). The plan for entering the market includes a local lawyer to handle
Korea's complex regulation, partnerships with local platforms because the *chaebol*
conglomerates dominate the economy, and marketing with K-pop idols, gamers and streamers.

Made as the final project of a course on **entrepreneurship in South and East Asia**, with a
lean canvas, market research and a pitch.
