## The place

The **Hadassah school for hospitalised children** works inside the children's surgery department
at Hadassah Ein Karem in Jerusalem. It serves children aged 3 to 18 from many cultures, with
different treatments and lengths of stay. Its teachers give educational and emotional support,
and prepare children for medical procedures. In 2022, about 41,500 operations were performed on
children and teenagers in Israel.

## Research

The research followed one surgery day hour by hour, through observations in the department and
conversations with teachers, parents and children. It covered the time from leaving home at 7:00 to
the preparation class, the doctor's tests, hours of free time, receiving a room, the pre-op room,
anaesthesia, the operating room, recovery and going home late at night.

What we heard:

- **Anaesthesia is the scariest part**: losing control.
- **The waiting is the hardest**: _“It feels like forever, just waiting.”_
- **Uncertainty is everywhere**: when will the doctor come, when will there be a room, when will
  the surgery start.
- **Parents need support too.** They are the key figure who explains things to the child, and
  sometimes they fall apart themselves.
- **Preparation helps.** A teacher who explains each station, shows the anaesthesia mask and
  breathes with the child builds a calmer, more positive memory.
- **After surgery there is rarely a chance to process the experience** with the school's staff.

## The gap

**Knowledge, time, experience.** Children need to know the stations on the way, the long hours
between the preparation class and the surgery need to be filled, and the whole day should leave a
positive memory.

## The game

**“Peak of Medicine”** is a board game whose squares are the real stations of a hospital day.
Players create their own die and character, roll to move, and roll a “luck” die that gives them a
**riddle**, a **task** or nothing:

- **Riddles** teach what to expect, such as what the anaesthesia mask is for or what to do if it
  hurts.
- **Tasks** invite play and feeling, such as giving a surgical tool a name or telling about a time
  a hug helped.
- The goal is to collect four victory points from the key stations: admission, preparation,
  anaesthesia, and food and drink.

The kit also holds a real first-aid kit and 3D-printed medical toys, so children get to know the
equipment with their own hands.

## Iterations

Three rounds of kits were made and tested with the school (4, then 5, then 7 kits). The first
showed the stay from three points of view: teacher, parent and child. The second added a teacher
card for each kit, put real equipment inside, merged the parent and child tracks into one, and
added a medal as a gift. The third made the kit smaller and easier to carry, merged the cards,
added pull-out tabs, improved the texts and added assembly instructions and a reminder to return
the kit.
