## The challenge

Most water-quality projects in the field rely on lab tests done by experts. Our challenge was to
let **women without formal education monitor their own water**, with simple tools they could use
at home.

## The process

1. **The pollutants.** We started by learning which pollutants are found in water, focusing on
   the main ones in **Kerala**, where the project began. For each, we looked at how it is tested,
   what range is acceptable, and the short- and long-term effects of being outside that range.
2. **Similar projects.** We looked for projects done in the field and by untrained teams.
3. **Home testing tools.** We searched for measuring tools that are simple to use, and weighed
   them by cost, test complexity and accuracy.

## Challenges

None of the team came from a related background, so the first third of the semester was spent
learning on our own. We also faced internet problems, and many companies had reduced or stopped
their activity, so it was hard to get quotes or detailed information about some devices. Good
communication and a will to understand each other got us through.

## What comes next

We laid the foundation for a project with a future:

- **A testing kit**, assembled from the list of tests we found.
- **A database** of test results, which is the project's main goal. The questionnaire needs to be
  shorter so people can keep using it every day.
- **An app** that collects data offline and sends it when there is a connection, since mobile
  use in India is growing but internet coverage is still partial. A paper booklet collected
  monthly is another option.
- In the end, a way to **purify the water**, not only to collect data about it.
