**A Bezalel guest seminar project · mentored by Guy Blender**
Team: Aya Dekel, Elisheva Shiri Decktor (product engineer), Tomi Boyarchik, Adi Kfiri and Amit Shahaf.

## The problem

Agriculture in the Central Arava produces a large amount of organic and inorganic waste, and
handling it comes with unique challenges. Every year the region generates an estimated
**60,000 tons of vegetable waste, 4,500 tons of date palm fronds and 2,000 tons of inorganic
waste** such as plastic sheeting. With few end-point solutions, much of it ends up in piles at the
edges of fields and villages, or is burned in the open air.

Solutions exist, but putting them in place requires significant systemic intervention and
outside resources.

## Research

Over three days we carried out **7 field tours, 8 interviews**, and reviewed **6 articles,
5 government documents and 10 press reports**. We traced the route each type of waste takes,
mapped the stakeholders involved, and plotted existing solutions from around the world by cost
and reach.

## Insights

- The region lacks infrastructure and end-point solutions for waste, and faces challenges of its own.
- Residents have come to accept the situation, and even to feel indifferent to it.
- Managing waste in the Arava requires ongoing education and awareness.

## The proposal

**Community mapping of waste hazards**, building a comprehensive, always-updated database
that mobilises the authorities to support end-point solutions.

The platform has two sides:

- **An augmented-reality game for children and teens.** Players photograph waste piles to
  collect monsters. Each capture records the waste's location and a photo, so the data is
  collected naturally, through play.
- **A professional interface for the regional environmental unit.** Reports appear on a map,
  classified by waste type, volume, location and how long they have been there. The unit can
  prioritise action and give regulators a clear picture of the region.

## Roadmap

A documentation and photography protocol (1 month) and aerial drone photography (1 month), then
app development (2 months), six months of data collection and analysis, and a community campaign
launched alongside a regulation document (2 months).
