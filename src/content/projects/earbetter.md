## The problem

In noisy places (classrooms, cafeterias, streets, stadiums) people with anxiety or sensitivity to
sound get overwhelmed. Noise-cancelling headphones help only partly: they block **everything**,
including the voice you want to hear and the sounds that keep you safe.

## Research

- **A survey of 38 people.** 95% experience anxiety, and 76% use noise-cancelling headphones,
  most of them to relieve anxiety, but the relief averages just 3.1/5. Sirens, repetitive small
  noises, heavy breathing and chewing bother people most. In their own words, they want comfort
  for long use, adjustable filtering, and to still hear speech.
- **A Reddit audit** of 127 posts and comments from anxiety communities (2020–2026). 110 describe
  sound triggering or worsening anxiety. Home and crowded places come up most, and general
  loudness, crowds and overlapping voices are the main culprits.
- **Literature** on soundscapes and wellbeing in hospitals, campuses and public spaces.

## The solution

**EarBetter** is a pair of adaptive headphones guided by the body:

- **Sensing.** Skin conductance (GSR) through an ESP32 board and heart rhythm (PPG) from a Muse 2
  headband feed a stress estimate. EEG frequency bands are the next step.
- **Selective hearing.** When stress rises, the audio pipeline softens background noise and lowers
  the volume. The goal is to reduce only the triggering sounds (a pen clicking, chewing) while
  keeping the lecturer's voice and safety sounds.
- **Hardware.** 3D-printed clips hold the GSR electrodes against the headphone, with an enclosure
  for the electronics.
- **A companion app.** A playful, non-clinical web app: choose a buddy, check in, switch between
  auto balancing and personal tune, keep a short diary, and see calm progress over time. **You can
  try the demo live** (button above). It runs entirely in your browser with simulated data.

_EarBetter is a wellness prototype, not a medical device._

## Testing the idea

The plan is a **randomised crossover study**: 15 participants, aged 14–21 with diagnosed anxiety,
try both EarBetter and traditional noise-cancelling headphones in a loud setting (a version of the
Trier Social Stress Test), then swap. The primary outcome is a drop in anxiety symptoms; the
secondary one is engagement with treatment. Stress is read from **skin conductance** (rises over a
personal calm baseline) and **heart rate variability** (RMSSD falls under stress).

## The pitch

EarBetter was pitched as a startup: a silicone cover that turns **the earbuds you already own** into
a biosensing device, at an estimated $299 a year, with a path from grants and university wellness
budgets to insurers and student health plans. I was the team's **CPO**, working with Itamar Avitan
(CEO), Alyssa Ignaco (CTO), Yuval Partok (CDO) and Lewis Núñez Severino (CSO), with Stephanie Foster,
MD, PhD, as clinical consultant.

## Where it started: Sorona Wav

EarBetter continues **Sorona Wav – Easy to hear again** (2023), a project I developed with
Prof. Reuven Ianconescu. It addressed the “cocktail party” problem for people with hearing loss:
following one voice among many. Sorona Wav used deep learning (a CNN and LSTM over Mel
spectrograms) for **blind source separation**, learning a mask that pulls each sound source out of
a mix, and was evaluated against a classic Wiener filter.

EarBetter takes the same idea of selective hearing and lets the body decide when to use it.
