## The problem

Grandparents aged 65+ often feel emotionally and physically distant from their families and
grandchildren, and their quality of life drops. Video calls are the usual answer for families
who live far apart, but many grandparents find it hard to talk with grandchildren who are always
on their phones.

## The idea

**FlowSense** is a different way for grandparents and grandchildren to communicate, without
words. Each has an aesthetic physical device connected to an app. Through lights, sounds and
vibration, they imitate each other's movements, pressing, stroking and moving the device the
same way. Moving in **synchrony** is known to make people feel closer. The games are
co-operative, not competitive: the two play together against the game.

## How we got there

We worked in the **Joy Ventures incubator** for wellbeing innovation, with the **Lean Startup**
and **Design Thinking** methods.

- **Research.** We started with boredom and moved on to loneliness, which affects more than a
  third of people over 45.
- **Interviews.** We spoke with 17 older adults and many experts, including psychotherapists,
  researchers, AgeTech companies, municipal staff and the TAU Entrepreneurship Center.
- **Persona.** Our focus narrowed to grandmothers aged 65 to 79 who live far from their
  grandchildren.
- **Ideation.** Two ideation rounds produced ideas such as a responsive plant, a digital pendant
  and a "sensory flower". The flower became FlowSense.

## Validation

We tested whether imitating each other's movements is enjoyable and brings people closer. 24
women aged 65 to 79 took part in two remote sessions over Zoom: 12 did a movement-imitation
activity, and 12 in a control group watched short videos. Both groups enjoyed it, and closeness
rose more in the movement group. Several participants said they would like to do it with their
grandchildren.

## Next steps

Our next steps were to bring grandchildren in as a second persona, run a validation with
grandmothers and grandchildren together, build a business model, and build the first prototype
with a designer, starting from the two-Arduino circuit.

I co-founded FlowSense with Gal Perlman and Alon Rosenbaum. I led the technology prototype and
the market research: persona, competitors and community.
